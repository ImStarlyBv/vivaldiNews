The United States and Iran concluded a critical round of indirect nuclear talks in Geneva on Thursday, with mediator Oman declaring "significant progress" but no deal reached — leaving the specter of U.S. military action firmly in place.

The negotiations, mediated by Omani Foreign Minister Sayyid Badr Albusaidi, brought together Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi and U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner for two sessions — morning and afternoon — at an undisclosed location in the Swiss city.

## Washington's Position

The Trump administration has maintained a firm stance: Iran must abandon all uranium enrichment capability and agree to discuss its ballistic missile program and support for regional armed groups. Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned that Iran is building missiles "designed solely to strike America" and called Tehran's refusal to include its missile program in negotiations a "major problem."

A senior U.S. official described the Geneva talks as "positive," while Vice President JD Vance stated, "I think we all prefer the diplomatic option" — a notable softening of rhetoric amid the largest U.S. military buildup near Iran in decades.

The U.S. has amassed significant naval and air assets in waters near the Islamic Republic, with the USS Gerald Ford carrier group set to arrive off the coast of Israel on February 27. The deployment signals Washington's readiness to act if diplomacy fails.

## The Diplomatic Gamble

Technical-level discussions are now scheduled to take place in Vienna next week, with Omani FM Badr Albusaidi traveling to Washington on Friday to meet with Vance and other officials. Many analysts view this round as the last chance before Trump could authorize military strikes.

The U.S. State Department also authorized the departure of non-emergency personnel and family members from Israel, while China issued an unprecedented warning to its citizens in Iran to "evacuate as soon as possible" — moves that underscore the gravity of the moment.

Despite the diplomatic language, the core impasse remains: Washington wants Iran to surrender its enrichment capabilities entirely, while Tehran insists on its right to peaceful nuclear energy and demands sanctions relief as a precondition.
