Iran entered the Geneva negotiations offering what officials described as new concessions, describing the Thursday talks as among the most serious diplomatic engagements between Tehran and Washington in years — while insisting on its fundamental rights.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi told state television after the session: "We reached agreement on some issues, and there are differences regarding some other issues." He characterized the talks as intense, serious, and constructive.

## Tehran's Framework

Iran's negotiating position centers on a clear demand: any agreement must separate nuclear from non-nuclear issues. A senior Iranian official told Reuters that a framework deal is achievable if Washington stops bundling Iran's ballistic missile program and regional influence into the nuclear file.

Tehran has long maintained that its nuclear program is entirely peaceful and that enrichment is a sovereign right under the Non-Proliferation Treaty. Iran offered what it described as "undefined new concessions" in exchange for the lifting of U.S. sanctions and recognition of its right to enrich uranium — a red line Tehran has never abandoned.

Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson stated before the talks that the country would "show flexibility," signaling a willingness to engage that contrasts with the Trump administration's maximalist demands.

## Under the Shadow of Threats

From Tehran's perspective, the negotiations are taking place under extreme duress. The unprecedented U.S. military buildup near Iran's borders is viewed not as leverage but as coercion — an attempt to force capitulation rather than achieve genuine compromise.

The Iranian delegation expressed particular frustration at Washington's insistence on including ballistic missiles in the talks. Tehran argues that its missile program is a defensive necessity, particularly given the military threats it faces. The demand to discuss missiles is seen in Iran as moving the goalposts from what was supposed to be a nuclear-focused negotiation.

Adding to the sense of urgency, China warned its citizens to evacuate Iran "as soon as possible," citing rising security risks — a move that Tehran views as reflecting external pressure rather than any internal threat.

Despite these pressures, Araqchi confirmed that another round of talks would take place "in less than a week," with technical discussions in Vienna, suggesting Iran remains committed to the diplomatic track even as it prepares for all scenarios.
