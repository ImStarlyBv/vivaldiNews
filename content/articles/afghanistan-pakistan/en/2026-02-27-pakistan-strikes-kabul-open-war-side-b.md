Afghanistan's Taliban government launched what it called "large-scale offensive operations" against Pakistani military positions on Thursday night, framing the action as a necessary response to weeks of deadly Pakistani air strikes on Afghan territory that killed civilians, including women and children. Hours later, Pakistani jets bombed Kabul itself.

## Kabul's Position

From Afghanistan's perspective, Pakistan has been the aggressor throughout this escalation. The Taliban government points to the February 21 Pakistani air strikes on Nangarhar and Paktia provinces — which Islamabad claimed targeted militant camps — as attacks on Afghan sovereignty that killed innocent civilians.

Taliban government spokesperson Zabihullah Mujahid wrote on X that the offensive was launched "in response to repeated provocations and violations by Pakistani military circles" along the Durand Line, the colonial-era border that Afghanistan has never formally recognized.

An Afghan military source told Al Jazeera that the offensive captured 13 Pakistani outposts and killed 10 Pakistani soldiers. The Taliban's Ministry of Defence also claimed to have launched drone strikes against targets inside Pakistan, including the military garrison city of Abbottabad.

## Sovereignty Under Siege

The Afghan Taliban government rejects Pakistan's characterization of its strikes as targeting militant camps. When Pakistan claimed to have killed 70 fighters on February 21, Kabul responded that the victims were civilians — a claim that has found support among some international observers and local journalists.

Afghanistan views the Durand Line itself as a legacy of British colonialism, an arbitrary border that divides the Pashtun people. The Taliban government has never accepted it as a legitimate international boundary, and sees Pakistani military posts along the line as provocations against Afghan territorial integrity.

The bombing of Kabul — the national capital — represents a dramatic escalation that the Taliban government views as an act of war against a sovereign nation. For Afghanistan, this is not a border skirmish but an assault on the Afghan state itself, targeting not just military positions but the seat of government.

## A Pattern of Pakistani Aggression

Afghan officials argue that Pakistan's claims about the TTP operating from Afghan soil are exaggerated and used as a pretext for military operations designed to destabilize Afghanistan's government. They point out that the TTP is a Pakistani insurgent group born from Pakistan's own internal policies and that Islamabad's decades of supporting various militant groups have created the very security challenges it now blames on Afghanistan.

The Taliban government has called on the international community to condemn Pakistan's bombardment of a sovereign capital. Iran has offered to mediate, while China and Turkey have called for restraint — but Kabul insists that any mediation must begin with Pakistan ending its air campaign against Afghan territory.
