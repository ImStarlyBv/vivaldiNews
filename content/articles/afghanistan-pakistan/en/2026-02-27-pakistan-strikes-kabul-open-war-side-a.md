Pakistan launched air strikes on the Afghan capital Kabul and the provinces of Kandahar and Paktika on Friday, after Afghanistan's Taliban government announced a major offensive against Pakistani military positions along the Durand Line the previous night. Pakistan's Defence Minister Khawaja Asif declared it was now "open war."

## Islamabad's Position

Pakistan frames its military operations as a defensive response to what it calls unprovoked Taliban aggression. On Thursday night, the Afghan Taliban announced "large-scale offensive operations" against Pakistani military posts along the border, claiming to have captured 13 outposts and killed 10 Pakistani soldiers — claims Islamabad has categorically denied.

Pakistan's Ministry of Information stated that its troops delivered an "immediate and effective response" across several sectors in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, including Chitral, Khyber, Mohmand, Kurram and Bajaur, reporting "heavy casualties on the Afghan side with multiple posts and equipment destroyed."

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif praised the armed forces, saying Pakistan's military is able to "crush" its foes. His spokesperson, Mosharraf Zaidi, insisted that no Pakistani outposts had been "captured or damaged" and that forces had "inflicted heavy losses" in response to what he called "unprovoked Taliban aggression."

## The Security Rationale

Pakistan's strikes come after months of escalating violence along the 2,611-kilometer border. Islamabad has long accused the Afghan Taliban government of harboring and supporting the Pakistani Taliban (TTP), which has carried out devastating attacks inside Pakistan, including a suicide bombing in Islamabad that killed dozens in recent weeks.

On February 21, Pakistan carried out air strikes on alleged TTP and ISIS-K training camps in eastern Afghanistan, claiming to have killed at least 70 fighters. Pakistan views these operations as essential to its national security, arguing that the Taliban government has failed — or refused — to prevent Afghan soil from being used as a launchpad for attacks against Pakistan.

Pakistan's Information Minister Atta Tarar reported that the country's anti-drone systems successfully intercepted Taliban drone attacks targeting the cities of Abbottabad, Swabi and Nowshera, underscoring what Islamabad sees as a direct military threat from across the border.

## International Response

Iran has offered to mediate, China has called for dialogue, and the foreign ministers of Saudi Arabia and Turkey have urged an end to hostilities in calls with their Pakistani counterpart. Former AP journalist Kathy Ganon described the situation as "a serious escalation" complicated by shifting alliances — notably Afghanistan's growing ties with India, Pakistan's historic rival.

Analysts tell the BBC that Pakistan is serious about "punishing" Afghanistan's leaders, but note that a prolonged conventional conflict would be devastating for both nations and the broader region.
