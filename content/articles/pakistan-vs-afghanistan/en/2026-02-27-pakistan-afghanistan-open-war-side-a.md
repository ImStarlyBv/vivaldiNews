Pakistan launched a major military operation against Afghanistan on February 27, 2026, with jets striking targets in Kabul, Kandahar and Paktia provinces. Defence Minister Khawaja Asif declared the two countries are now in a state of "open war," marking the most dramatic escalation between the nuclear-armed neighbour and the Taliban-governed state since the group returned to power in 2021.

## Islamabad's Justification

Pakistan's military framed the strikes as a necessary act of self-defence after months of escalating cross-border attacks by militants operating from Afghan soil. Pakistani officials say the Taliban government has failed — or refused — to prevent armed groups, particularly the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP), from using Afghan territory to launch attacks against Pakistani security forces and civilians.

The Pakistani military reported that Afghan forces had attacked Pakistani border positions overnight, prompting the retaliatory strikes. Islamabad has long demanded that the Taliban government crack down on TTP militants sheltering in Afghanistan, a demand Kabul has consistently deflected.

## A Pattern of Escalation

The strikes represent the culmination of months of deteriorating relations. Pakistan has conducted smaller-scale cross-border operations in the past, but targeting Kabul itself marks a dramatic escalation. Pakistan's military establishment views the Taliban's refusal to act against the TTP as an existential threat, given the group's campaign of bombings and attacks inside Pakistan that have killed hundreds in recent years.

The international community has urged restraint, with multiple nations calling for de-escalation. Pakistan maintains that it exhausted all diplomatic options before resorting to military action, and that the operation is focused on militant infrastructure rather than Afghan civilians.

Pakistan's Foreign Ministry stated that the country reserves the right to protect its sovereignty and citizens, and that the operation will continue until the threat from cross-border terrorism is neutralised.
