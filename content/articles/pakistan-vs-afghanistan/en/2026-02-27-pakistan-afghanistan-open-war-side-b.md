Afghanistan's Taliban government condemned Pakistan's airstrikes on Kabul, Kandahar and Paktia provinces as a brazen act of aggression against a sovereign nation, declaring its own full military operation in response. Taliban security forces deployed anti-aircraft defences near the Torkham border crossing and mobilised troops along the Durand Line.

## Kabul's Position

The Taliban government rejects Pakistan's characterisation of the strikes as counter-terrorism. Afghan officials argue that Pakistan is violating Afghan sovereignty and attacking a country that has already suffered decades of foreign intervention. The strikes on the capital Kabul — home to millions of civilians — represent a particularly inflammatory escalation.

Afghanistan's Defence Ministry reported that the strikes killed civilians and damaged residential areas, calling on the international community to condemn what it described as Pakistan's war crimes. The Taliban's spokesperson emphasised that Afghanistan never authorised or supported attacks on Pakistani territory and that the TTP is an internal Pakistani problem.

## The Durand Line Dispute

At the heart of the conflict lies the Durand Line, the colonial-era border that Afghanistan has never formally recognised. The Taliban government, like every Afghan government before it, views Pakistani border infrastructure — including fencing and fortification — as an encroachment on Afghan territory and a division of Pashtun communities on both sides.

From Kabul's perspective, Pakistan's claim that it is fighting terrorism is a pretext for asserting control over the border region and pressuring the Taliban government into political submission. Afghan officials note that Pakistan supported the Taliban during their insurgency only to turn hostile once they refused to serve as Islamabad's proxy in power.

The Taliban government has called for international mediation and urged Muslim-majority nations to condemn Pakistan's aggression. Taliban forces, including fighters who spent two decades battling the world's most powerful militaries, have vowed to defend every inch of Afghan soil.
