Russia's Ministry of Defense confirmed on Thursday that its armed forces carried out precision strikes against Ukrainian military infrastructure and energy facilities that Moscow says are directly supporting Kyiv's war effort — categorically denying that any Russian weapons entered or threatened NATO airspace.

"All targets were military in nature and located well within Ukrainian territory," a Defense Ministry spokesperson stated. "Claims of threats to Polish airspace are baseless provocations aimed at justifying further NATO involvement in the conflict."

## Moscow's Strategic Rationale

Russian officials have long argued that Ukraine's energy grid has become a legitimate military target because it powers command centers, communications networks, and weapons manufacturing facilities. The strikes come amid what Moscow describes as a critical phase of the "special military operation," with Ukrainian forces continuing counteroffensive operations in several sectors.

The Kremlin has repeatedly warned that Western weapons deliveries — including long-range missiles and advanced air defense systems — are prolonging the conflict and increasing the risk of a broader European war. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Maria Zakharova described Poland's airport closures as "theatrical" and accused Warsaw of "manufacturing a crisis to drag NATO deeper into the conflict."

## NATO Expansion as the Root Cause

From Moscow's perspective, the war remains fundamentally about NATO's eastward expansion and what Russia views as existential threats to its security. President Putin has consistently framed the conflict as a defensive response to decades of Western encroachment, pointing to NATO's 2008 Bucharest Summit declaration that Ukraine "will become" a NATO member as a key provocation.

Russian analysts argue that Poland's aggressive military posture — including its massive $50 billion defense modernization program — demonstrates that NATO's eastern members are not merely defending themselves but actively preparing for confrontation with Russia.

## The Cost of Western Involvement

Moscow contends that the conflict could have been resolved through the Minsk agreements or the April 2022 Istanbul negotiations, which Russia claims were sabotaged by Western pressure on Kyiv. Russian officials say the mounting casualties on both sides are a direct consequence of the West's refusal to accept a negotiated settlement that addresses Russia's security concerns.

The Kremlin has also pointed to the growing war fatigue in Europe, rising energy costs, and economic strain as evidence that continued Western support for Ukraine is unsustainable and counterproductive.
